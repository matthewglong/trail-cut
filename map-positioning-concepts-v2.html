TRAILCUT/Map Positioning · Spacing Studies

The current modal centers three frames inside a row that's dictated by the widest aspect. The other two columns inherit dead air. Three premises for fixing it — each starts by questioning a different assumption: that the modal needs a header band, that it needs chrome at all, or that all three frames need to be visible at once.

v02.00 · 03 studies
01

Workbench· frames are the surface

Pattern Chromeless triptych

Strip the header band. Strip the footer. Strip every internal margin. Each column's width is proportional to its aspect ratio, so all three frames render at the same effective scale and the dead air collapses. The only chrome is brand-stamp top-left, close top-right, and per-frame controls tucked into the bracket corners.

The trick
Column widths are proportional to aspect ratio (1.78 : 0.80 : 0.56). Each frame fills its column edge-to-edge. The three frames render at identical pixel-per-output-pixel scale, which kills the wasted negative space without resorting to letterboxing.
Chrome budget
One brand stamp, one close button. Per-frame: ratio label, mode pill, reset — each parked inside a bracket corner so it reads as part of the lens, not a toolbar. No header band, no footer.
Cost
The 9:16 column is narrow at small window widths. We could either set a minWidth of 1100 on the modal, or let the row scroll horizontally below that threshold. The latter feels right for a creative tool.
02

Spotlight· one frame at a time, others in your peripheral vision

Pattern Focus + thumbnails

Admit that you can only really evaluate one crop at a time. Promote the active ratio to the full canvas; keep the other two as live thumbnails on the left so they're never out of sight. The header becomes the ratio's own title, which means no wasted top band — it's pulling its weight.

The trick
The current modal asks you to compare three crops; in practice users tweak one at a time. Promote the act of tweaking. The thumbnails on the rail are live — every drag in the spotlight propagates so the other crops never go stale.
Chrome budget
The "header band" earns its keep because it's the active ratio's own identity — replacing what was a generic title. Mode/reset controls sit beside it. The footer is keyboard hints (optional; can hide on hover).
Cost
Loses true side-by-side. If a user wants to A/B 16:9 vs 9:16 they can do it through the thumbnails, but the comparison is unequal-scale. Not ideal if "compare" is the primary job.
03

Atlas· not a modal — a positioning mode

Pattern Full-bleed editing mode

Stop pretending this is a dialog. The user wants to position frames against a map; show them the map. Three frames float over the project's actual route view; controls live in floating chips attached to each. Enter with a button, leave with esc. The "wasted space" problem doesn't exist because there's no card to waste space inside.

The trick
Reframe the task as a mode, not a dialog. The app's main canvas becomes the substrate; the three frames levitate over it. There's no rectangle to fight against, so the question of "is the modal too sparse" never comes up.
Chrome budget
A single floating toolbar pill at the top: brand stamp, ratio tabs, esc hint. Each frame carries its own floating chip with ratio, mode, reset. Chrome is dissolved into the canvas.
Cost
Heaviest implementation — needs a "positioning mode" state in the app shell, not just a portal modal. Also: showing the actual map behind requires the map view to be the literal background of the editor when entering this mode.
